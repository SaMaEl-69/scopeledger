Core calculations still reproduce the required example: approved fee **8,000**, actual cost **2,000**, remaining cost **3,200**, additional **8 × 65**, target **35%**, proposed fee **800** produces **35% current**, **28.5% absorbed**, **800 change floor**, **800 project-restoration fee**, and **35% agreed**. Minimum positive recommendations round upward. An underpriced baseline distinguishes protecting the new work from restoring the full project; a profitable baseline can have no additional restoration charge. Unknown estimates remain unknown, negative margins stay visible, and credits remain deliberate revenue reductions with a reason.

Approval remains tied to the exact current revision. Relevant change/baseline edits invalidate active approval and preserve prior terms. Reconciliation is exact-once, the incurred/future split equals gross additional cost, and savings reduce only eligible future forecast. Included changes and their invoices reuse the preserved before-baseline, including exchanges whose removal would no longer fit the current forecast. Archive/trash cannot bypass unreconciled commitments or the demo allowance.

Duplication uses explicit carry-over choices, fresh IDs, reused client identity and draft status requiring fresh approval. It leaves source approvals, invoices, payments, reminders and history intact. Optional copied revision references cannot become active approval. Scenarios and reusable assumptions apply deliberately without changing the original baseline or financial estimates; alternatives remain independent of the active decision.

Client transfers remain an allowlist. Loaded rates, internal costs, hours, margins/targets, private client/payment notes, raw approval evidence and comparisons do not enter the payload or shared HTML. Issued public values are immutable when current client, project, agency or logo settings change. Manual tax and balances use exact decimal arithmetic; partial payment uses the document total including tax, rather than project contract revenue. Credits and outgoing settlements stay separate from invoices and received cash. Void operations retain history and require a reason.

## Data and operating limits

Unissued document settings are not saved snapshots. The open application retains the **50 most recently edited** project/change/type contexts in memory, with explicit UI wording; reload clears them. Switching types/views preserves recent text, while replacing a draft confirms first. Confirming a snapshot adds immutable history and its backup contents. Successful workspace replacement clears temporary draft caches. This working-form bound does not cap issued documents or paid project features.

Valid imported records stay unchanged, including future-dated history whose clock/evidence requires review. Current public previews may withhold an unsupported fractional-cent amount or doubtful approval claim; existing issued snapshots are never rebuilt or rewritten. Local backup size and browser storage remain practical limits; failures must retain recoverable edits and explain the actual save state.

The toolkit needs no recurring AI service. Logo preparation stays local, validates PNG/JPEG, fits proportionally and bounds source/fitted size. Immutable snapshots intentionally retain their then-current public branding; many logo-bearing snapshots increase backup size. Paid renderer, database, hosting and support economics still require the owner's production capacity and policy decisions. No promised paid feature was silently removed to solve operating cost.

Manual payment tracking is not payment processing or bank reconciliation; manual tax arithmetic is not tax advice or invoice-compliance certification. Customer-facing wording still requires review: the allowlist cannot identify a secret someone deliberately pastes into a permitted public field.
